Configure by equipment, not by trigger.

A critique of the 28 September configuration handoff (the cfg-console-v4 console and its PRD), and a proposal that keeps the approved data model but changes what an admin sees and does — so a plant can be set up in an hour by someone who has never read the PRD.

In ten lines

The equipment tree is the console. An admin configures a machine, a stage or the plant — never an abstract list of 300 triggers. Every alert, schedule and job lives on the thing it is about.
Limits live on the sensor. Caution · Minor · Major · Emergency thresholds are a property of the instrument. An alert uses them by default; two alerts never disagree about one probe.
Three plain things to add: an alert (something is wrong), a schedule (planned work: routine, preventive maintenance, calibration), and condition-based work (the plant asks for a job). Underneath, all three are the PRD's one Trigger entity.
Nobody draws who is told. Every alert and schedule shows, resolved from the real roster, who hears now, who hears when nothing starts, who hears when it is overdue. It is a preview, not a form.
Clocks are derived and shown as a consequence — one line, not a section. The two numbers the Notifications Brief lets a Technical Admin change sit behind one link.
Standard sets replace the template library. A machine of a known type shows "3 alerts and 2 schedules not added yet — review and add", bound to its own tags, landing as drafts.
Setup is a guided path, not a validator. Five steps with a progress bar say whether alerts reach a human, which sensors lack limits, which machines lack alerts or a maintenance programme, which causes lack a fix, and offer a one-click answer for each.
Flows keep the v4 editor's model (an answer owns its destination; a conclusion names a cause; a cause names its fix). Causes are a tab inside Flows, not a fourth library.
Maintenance mode, PLC set points and cycles are visible where they bite: on the sensor row and the alert form, so monitoring limits, control set points and batch phases never contradict each other silently.
No spec vocabulary on screen. No rung, band, promotion, attribution, observation poll, lead parameter, §-numbers or AC ids. A "Show the model underneath" toggle reveals the PRD entity behind every card for engineers.

What the handoff gets wrong

The PRD is careful and most of its decisions are right: one entity, derived clocks, role-based routing, the freeze on triggers with open records, PPM as a scheduled task, sensor faults as their own path. The problem is the surface built on it. The console renders the specification instead of hiding it. Concretely:

1. The trigger form is the PRD with input boxes

Opening DO Low — Aeration Zone 2 in the console shows nine numbered sections (§1, §2, §2b, §3, §3b, §4, §5, §5b, §12), a 130-word essay under the Outcome selector, and roughly 1,500 words of helper prose before the footer. Acceptance-criteria ids appear in product copy ("Pinned at 1 — AC-ESC-11.3", "before FR-COND-07"). An admin who wants to say tell someone when DO drops below 2 is asked to understand bands, promotion, observation polls, resolution polls, subject versus observation, lead parameters and context readings first.

The PRD itself says half the dead ends in the console lead to equipment configuration. That is the tell: the console is organised around the wrong noun.

2. It is organised by trigger, when admins think by machine

The primary surface is a 39-row list filtered by Outcome, with a scope bar, three tabs and a banner explaining scope on every one of four libraries. Nobody at a plant asks "show me all triggers"; they ask "what watches Blower-1?" and "does the MBR have a CIP schedule?". Goal 13 already made the Equipment Card the operator's centre; the config console should mirror it rather than sit beside it as a different mental model.

3. One threshold, three homes

A DO limit can be authored on the sensor (zone defaults), in the formula library (a SignalCondition), and on the trigger (a band). The prototype shows all three on one screen: a band rail with RANGE MIN / MAX, a formula dropdown of 35 entries, and a note that the sensor has no zone set. One number should have one home. The PRD's own §9.2 points at it — the sensor.

4. The form asks what the system already knows

It states "Observed on Aeration Tank-2 in Biological, because that is where the formula's sensor is" and then asks the admin to choose a subject with a three-button question and two paragraphs of consequence. If you are on Blower-1's page, the alert is about Blower-1. If you are on Biological, it is about the stage. Position in the tree answers the question.

5. Card design leaks into configuration

"What the card shows" (lead parameter, up to three context readings, display limit) is a decision about the operator card's rendering, defaulted correctly by the alert's own sensor 95% of the time. It does not belong in the alert form. Achievements as a peer form mode hide four-fifths of the form and inflate every other mode with a helper explaining why.

6. It contradicts the Notifications Brief, which wins

  • Brief §4.1: "A Technical Admin may change [response time and deadline] on a rule." The console renders both read-only with a paragraph on why nobody may.
  • Brief §5: a person's settings are four rows plus quiet hours. The console's Roles page still offers a "Which moments?" checklist and a channel block per person.
  • Brief §6: admins set roster, quiet hours, hooter, the two clock numbers, and a technical-alerts toggle. Nothing else. The console's Site Settings spec still carries shift windows and a night override.

7. No path for a new plant

Day one at a new site is an empty trigger list and a template library in three tabs. The right day one is: equipment exists → its archetype says what it needs → the standard set is offered → the admin checks the numbers and activates. The "Ready to add" tab is the seed of this idea, buried two clicks deep.

8. Maintenance is a validator warning, not a programme

Addendum A folds PPM into scheduled Tasks (right), then surfaces coverage as a warning row in §12. An admin should see, on the machine, "its type expects a mechanical PM — none set", and on the plant, "14 of 17 machines have a programme". The Maintenance tool already proves how little config maintenance needs: a checklist per equipment type, a cadence, a team.

9. Reference pages and a state bar in the navigation

Record lifecycle, Open & close paths, Screen states, Design notes are handoff material, not product. The empty/loading/saving bar is a prototype affordance that reads as a toolbar.

Principles

These are the design rules the prototype is held to. Each one is checkable against a screen.

  1. One home per fact. Limits on the sensor. Schedules on the machine. People on the roster. A fact never has two editors that can disagree.
  2. Recognition over recall. The plant's own tree, names and sensors are the navigation. No library of abstract things to search.
  3. Defaults do the work. A standard set for the machine type, limits from the sensor, clocks from severity and criticality, closing rule from the entry limit. The admin confirms; they rarely author.
  4. Show the consequence, not the theory. "Told now: Dheeraj, Nagesh, Devid — WhatsApp" beats a paragraph about rung 1. A "try a reading" box beats a section on evaluation semantics.
  5. Plain words, one line. Every helper is one sentence a plant lead would say aloud. Spec words stay in the model overlay.
  6. Progressive disclosure. Hold time, gating, cycle phase, tolerance days, clock overrides are behind "Advanced" links. The common case is five fields.
  7. Nothing fires by surprise. Everything added from a standard set is a draft. A draft never fires. Activate runs every check, and the checks read as a checklist, not an essay.
  8. Reversible. Drafts, undo on delete, switch off instead of delete when records are open, pause instead of delete on schedules.
  9. Readiness over validation. The home page answers "is this plant set up?" with links to the place that fixes each gap.
  10. The model is one toggle away. Engineers flip "Show the model underneath" and every card names the PRD entity and fields it maps to. The vocabulary problem is solved by layering, not by choosing.

How it fits together

Configuration is the left half of a loop the rest of the product already defines. Everything an admin sets here has exactly one downstream consumer.

Where the other modules plug in

  • Equipment Card (Goal 13). The tree, archetypes, criticality tier, duty/standby and the "filed on one node, rolls up" rule are shared. The Lead's six-field cadence edit on the card and the console's schedule sheet write the same record — one store, two surfaces.
  • Maintenance tool. Its checklist-per-equipment-type becomes an action flow "for" that type; its Scheduled / Breakdown reasons become schedules and equipment-fault reports; its holds become the Blocked path; "Put in Maintenance" becomes Maintenance Mode, which pauses the machine's alerts. Its Excel PPM import stays as the way to seed schedules at migration.
  • Notifications. Consumed, never configured. The "who is told" block on every alert and schedule is the brief's tables resolved against the roster. People & alerts holds the only admin levers the brief allows.
  • Roles. Technical Admin owns Equipment and Flows; People Admin owns People & alerts; Global Admin owns Standard sets; a Lead edits in-house PM cadence in place. One new permission line, approve.schedule, carries the Lead's grant.
  • Remote Control. A sensor's PLC set point is shown beside its alert limits, read-only, with a nudge when an alert limit sits inside the control band. Maintenance-mode and group-control hooks stay on the card.
  • Insights+ cycles. The UF/SBR/MBR cycle-and-phase config already designed there is the missing input for cycle-bound issues. A machine with cycles shows them; an alert can be limited to a phase and closes at the boundary. This answers the PRD's open item 5 with something that exists.
  • Sensor Health and the Internal Tool. A reported or detected sensor fault pauses the sensor's alerts and shows a paused count on the sensor row; calibration and cleaning cadences on the sensor sheet generate calibration tasks, in the vendor's name where a contract exists.
  • Formula builder. Kept, demoted: a compound condition ("DO low AND blower running") is an Advanced option on an alert, and a computed sensor (RO recovery) is an ordinary row on the sensor table with an expression. The library still exists in the data model; it is not a navigation item.

Where this departs from the PRD and the handoff, and where it does not

Departures, stated plainly

  1. The formula library is not a navigation item. Thresholds are authored on sensors; compound conditions are an Advanced option inside an alert. The library remains in the model and can be exposed for migration and power users under Equipment, but it is not how an admin thinks.
  2. No Outcome selector. Three named actions replace a mode switch. The PRD's argument for one entity is about the model, not the form.
  3. No subject question. Tree position decides the subject. The observed-versus-subject distinction is preserved: a stage-level alert shows "read on AT-2".
  4. No scope bar on site surfaces. Global is a persona with its own Standard sets page. A site admin sees recommendations, not a library.
  5. Achievements leave the form. They are a list on the plant page.
  6. Card display settings leave the form. The lead reading is the alert's sensor; context readings are a card-design default per archetype.
  7. Response time and deadline are overridable per alert, behind a link, because the Notifications Brief says so and the brief wins. The list marks such alerts as exceptions.
  8. Subject gains a group level, because the Equipment Card's duty/standby sets and the blower-group task need it.
  9. Cycles are a config object, reusing the Insights+ cycle design, instead of an interim per-trigger chronic threshold.
  10. Reference pages leave the nav. They belong in the handoff bundle, which this document is.

Kept on purpose

  • One entity, three outcomes, one open record per trigger, the freeze on open records, drafts that never fire.
  • Severity from the limit crossed; promotion and silent demotion; peak severity for metrics.
  • Derived clocks for Issues (severity) and Tasks (tier × cadence); the ladder fixed by role; Emergency with no settings; Start as acknowledgement.
  • PPM as a scheduled Task with work type, due basis, calendar anchor, meter floor, vendor marking; Lead's six-field grant; coverage as warnings.
  • Sensor faults as their own path, pausing alerts, never counted; valid range on the sensor; reading states.
  • Flows v4 (answer owns destination), causes global-only, publish validators as two grades.
  • Attribution additive; events on one node rolling up; the 24-month audit.

What to build first

  1. Equipment page with sensors, limits and alerts. This is the whole value: one machine, its limits, its alerts, who is told. It reuses the equipment module's tree and the existing trigger and condition tables.
  2. Schedules and condition-based work on the same page, plus the Lead's in-place sheet. Migrate the Maintenance tool's PPM import into it.
  3. Plant setup checklist — cheap, because every check already exists as a validator or a coverage rule.
  4. People & alerts — mostly read-only, on top of the roster the User Center already has.
  5. Flows — the v4 editor is sound; restyle its shell to match and add the Causes tab.
  6. Standard sets for Global Admins, with roll-out as drafts. Until then, a site can still add everything by hand.

Using the prototype

  • Everything is in memory. Reset data in the top bar restores the starting state.
  • Sign in as switches persona: Technical Admin (full equipment config), People Admin (roster and site defaults), Lead (read-only, except in-house PM cadence), Global Admin (adds Standard sets).
  • ? Help in the top bar opens a panel that follows the screen and the step: what to do, why, and what happens if you skip. It also holds the three demo switches: make the next save fail, start from an empty plant, show the model underneath.
  • Good paths to walk: turn on "Start from an empty plant" and follow the five setup steps. Or: Equipment → Air Blower 1 → Add → an alert on Motor current, and try a reading of 50 on the review step. Or: sign in as the Lead → Air Blower 1 → Bearing service.
  • Not built here: the operator app, the equipment card itself, the notification pipeline, real persistence.
