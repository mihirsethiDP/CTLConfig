1 · Purpose and status

This is the build to start now. Close the Loop as a whole is not fully decided, so this doc draws a line: everything inside the configuration console is specified to the field, and everything that is still being decided is a named placeholder with an owner. Developers build the console in full. Placeholders are modelled so the console does not have to change shape when the decisions land.

The prototype at prototype.html is the reference for behaviour: five plants seeded at different depths, four personas, every add, edit and delete working, every placeholder shown as a dashed chip where it bites. Where this doc and the prototype disagree, the prototype wins on behaviour and this doc wins on contracts.

What the console is. One configuration per plant, in the hierarchy plant → unit process → control group → machine. It holds readings (from SCADA, entered by a person, or computed by a formula), limits, alerts, schedules and rounds, manual overrides, and the library check that compares the library's perfect picture with what the plant actually has. It reads the library; it never edits it. People, roles and escalation live in User Center and are not in the console. It compiles the plant to the shape the rules engine consumes.
Helpers for every end user. Every screen has a "How this works ›" link and every drawer a "What do these fields mean? ›" link. Both open the help panel: the purpose of the screen, who may change what, how to do the common things, what every field means, the terms used, and the pitfalls. The content ships as assets/help.js and is part of the build, not an afterthought. Chips carry tooltips; severity tiers explain who is told; fields carry examples; every validation message is a sentence that says what to change.
What it is not. Not the library (a separate build with its own authoring). Not the rules engine, the notification service, the operator app, the analytical engine that evaluates formulas, or the event log. The console produces their inputs and names what it cannot yet produce.

The rule between them. The console reads the library through one interface (section 8) and never writes to it. Everything inherited carries source: "library" and a templateRef; the moment a person edits it, it becomes source: "custom" and the library leaves it alone. The library build owns propagation: when a template changes, items that still follow it change. The console only has to keep the reference intact, and it does.

Why the console does not wait for the library. It ships with a stub implementing the same interface from a JSON file (the prototype's assets/library.js). The console cannot tell the difference, so it can be built, tested and used on day one. When the library build lands, the stub is swapped.

3 · Fundamental architecture

Principles the code must keep

  1. One configuration per plant. Every reading, alert and schedule sits on one subject in plant → unit process → control group → machine.
  2. The engine watches readings, nothing else. A SCADA tag, a value a person enters and a formula's result have the same shape, so an alert never cares where its value came from.
  3. Limits live on the reading. An alert uses them or overrides them for itself.
  4. The library offers, the console inherits, people override. Inherited items keep their template reference; Reset to library goes back. The library check lists every miss between the library's picture and the plant, and lets a miss be recorded as not installed.
  5. Severity is the console's whole say in who is told. People, roles and the ladder are User Center's. A stage, group or machine can be muted or put under maintenance by hand, until a date.
  6. Nothing with records is deleted, everything is audited, the export is the whole plant. Delete archives; every change that can wake someone up is logged with before and after; the engine reads the export and never the console's tables.

5 · Screens and acceptance criteria

The shell is the DigitalPaani app's: navy top bar with the persona switch, breadcrumb, the legend strip, a lede sentence, stat tiles, a banner with the next action, a search box, uppercase group headers, white row cards with title, sub-line links and chips on the right. Phone width: cards stack, chips wrap, no horizontal scroll.

5.1 Plants #/plants

  • Lists every plant the user may see, grouped by workspace, with machine, alert and schedule counts and chips: equipment, limits coverage, alerts, schedules, reach, and Live | Ready to go live | Draft.
  • Add plant (Site Admin): name, type, capacity, workspace, code, and optionally "start from a drawing" SCADA import. From a drawing: zones become unit processes with a detected profile, machines and widgets become machines and readings, two machines of one type in one zone become a control group with duty and standby.
  • Edit plant: the same fields. Delete plant: confirm, cascade everything at the plant, audited.
  • Search by name. Tiles: plants, machines, alerts active, share of items that follow the library, plants live, placeholders open.

5.2 Overview #/plant/:id/overview

  • Tiles: unit processes, machines, readings with limits of readings that need them, alerts active, schedules active, share that follows the library.
  • One "Next" banner computed in this order: no machines → untyped machines → items ready to inherit → readings without limits → nobody reached → drafts → go live → nothing waiting. Each links to its tab or offers Inherit everything | Choose.
  • "What the library offers" card: ready, already inherited, blocked (with why), placeholder counts. Inherit everything applies every ready item in up to three passes (formulas wait for their inputs). Choose opens a picker grouped by level with everything ticked.
  • "Placeholders at this plant" card lists the open decisions with owners.

5.3 Equipment #/plant/:id/equipment

  • Grouped by unit process; within each, control groups first (navy chip), then machines. A machine's row: name, tag, duty or standby, its group, type or Type not in library, tier, counts, gaps (no type, nothing watched, no programme), chips with Edit and Delete.
  • Add, edit, delete unit process (profile from the library). Deleting cascades its groups, machines, readings, alerts and schedules.
  • Add, edit, delete control group: name, unit process, members ticked from that unit process's machines. Ticking moves a machine between groups. Deleting a group removes its own readings and the alerts on them; the machines stay, ungrouped.
  • Add, edit, delete machine: tag (unique at the plant), name, unit process, control group, library type, tier with reason when it differs from the library, duty or standby of a sibling. Choosing a type for an untyped machine sets its tier to the library's.
  • ＋ Sensor tag on the plant header, on a unit process header, on a group row and on a machine row opens the reading drawer with that subject preset and SCADA as the input. Sensors from the drawing arrive on the machine, on the unit process (zone sensors, pipe sensors with no machine at either end) or on the plant.
  • Manual override in the edit drawer of a unit process, a group or a machine: Running normally | Alerts muted | Under maintenance, with an until-date and a reason, audited with who and since when. Rows show the override; alerts under it show Muted; schedules under maintenance show Paused. The nearest override up the chain wins. Validation: reason and date required, date not in the past.
  • Search by name, tag or type.

5.4 Readings and limits #/plant/:id/readings

  • Grouped by subject: the whole plant, then each unit process (its own readings, its control groups' readings, its machines' readings). Columns: reading and tag, kind, values from (SCADA | Entered: cadence and by whom, missed severity | Formula: expression and window), sample value, valid range, limits with Follows library | Custom, Edit, Delete.
  • Add, edit, delete reading. The drawer: where it lives (any of the four levels), values come from (SCADA | a person enters it | a formula; a reading imported from SCADA cannot change input), name, tag (made by the console when blank: MAN-…, CALC-…), unit, kind, the input-specific section (7), valid range, limits with direction and four tiers, sample value.
  • Every group header has ＋ Sensor tag, adding a SCADA reading on that subject. From the Library check, the same drawer opens prefilled from the library's definition (name, tag prefix, unit, kind, valid range, direction).
  • Deleting a reading removes every alert that watches it; a formula that used it loses that input and shows as invalid until rebound.
  • Banner: readings without limits, readings that would not pass validation, counts of data inputs Manual entry screen and formulas Analytical engine.

5.5 Alerts #/plant/:id/alerts

  • Grouped by unit process (a control group's or machine's alert sits under its unit process) plus "About the whole plant". Row: name, severity tiers, the sentence (reading, direction, entry limit, how it closes, Lead's check, clock overrides), chips: flow reference Flows, Follows library | Custom, Open records · frozen, status, Switch off | Activate, Edit, Delete.
  • Add, edit, delete alert. The drawer in five numbered sections: 1 which reading (subject, then the reading: on the subject first, then elsewhere at the plant); 2 when it is a problem (the reading's own limits or custom tiers, hold minutes; for an on/off contact, the severity); 3 how it closes (the reading comes back past a value for n minutes, or the operator declares it fixed); 4 what the operator runs (a library flow reference, a Lead's check); 5 overrides (start-within and fix-within hours, defaulted from the severity). A live sentence restates the whole alert and names who is told at once.
  • Activate refuses with the first validation sentence. Saving as active with a gap keeps it as a draft and says why. Delete archives when the alert has or had records.
  • Reset to library, on a custom alert that began in the library, replaces it with the library's current version; a version with records is archived rather than removed. The same button exists on schedules and on inherited readings.
  • An alert whose subject, or whose subject's group or stage, is overridden shows Muted with the reason and does not fire until the override ends; the export carries suppressedBy.
  • Inherit… opens the picker filtered to alerts. Search by name or subject.

5.6 Schedules and rounds #/plant/:id/schedules

  • Grouped by the whole plant, then unit process. Row: name, work type chip, Statutory, cadence (and run-hours), counted from, next due, performed by, Lead sign-off, the probe it calibrates, hours, chips: steps reference Flows, prevents Causes, Follows library | Custom, status, Pause | Activate, Edit, Delete.
  • Add, edit, delete schedule. Drawer: 1 what work, on what (plant, unit process, control group, machine; routine | preventive | calibration with its probe); 2 how often (calendar | run-hours | whichever first, every n unit, run-hours and the meter, counted from fixed dates or the last completion, next due, grace days); 3 who and how (our team or a vendor, statutory, Lead sign-off, hours, parts); 4 steps and the cause it prevents (library references).
  • A Lead may change only the cadence of in-house preventive maintenance; everything else is read-only for a Lead.

5.7 Library check #/plant/:id/coverage

  • The funnel, per plant. Tiles: sensors the library expects, installed, missing, recorded as not installed, library items blocked by a miss, items offered and not taken.
  • One card per machine (and per stage and for the plant): a table of the sensor tags the library expects for its type (tag, unit, kind, and which alerts, run-hours schedules and calibrations need it) against the sensor tags installed as SCADA reports them: Installed | Missing | Not installed with reason, who, when. No value is read from the plant; it is configuration against configuration.
  • Missing → ＋ Add the sensor tag opens the reading drawer prefilled from the library's definition, on that machine. Missing → Not installed… asks for a reason and records it; the library items that need the sensor become Skipped instead of Blocked, and the export names the gap under knownGaps. Expect it again reverses it.
  • Under each card, what the library offers for that subject: Offered (with Inherit), Blocked (with what it needs), Skipped (with the recorded reason), Placeholder (with the pending decision).
  • A machine without a type or a stage without a profile is called out: the library cannot say what it should have.
  • People are not here. The console shows nothing about the roster, the ladder or quiet hours; they live in User Center.

5.8 Review and go live #/plant/:id/review

  • Checklist in tab order. Blocking (red): machines exist, every machine typed, every active alert valid, no drafts. Non-blocking (amber): the library check settled, no manual override in force, readings valid, limits complete, every machine with a reading watched, maintenance programme complete, nothing left to inherit.
  • Banner: Not live yet (n things) → Almost (activate drafts) → Ready (Go live) → Live (Back to draft). Activate all drafts activates only those that pass validation.
  • Download the hand-off JSON (section 9). The same button sits in the plant header.

5.9 Audit #/plant/:id/audit · #/audit

  • Per plant and across the estate: when, who, plant, what, detail; searchable; newest first; 24 months.

5.10 Library #/library · read-only

  • Shows what the console reads: plant types with rounds, data inputs, formulas and milestones (placeholder); unit process profiles with rounds, inputs, formulas, pairs; equipment types with expected readings, alerts (connection-based ones as placeholders), schedules (condition-based ones as placeholders). Nothing is editable here; the Global Admin's authoring is the library build.

6 · Validation rules

Each rule is a sentence the user sees. Save refuses with the first one for machines and readings; alerts and schedules may be saved as drafts with gaps, never activated with them.

EntityRule
MachineTag and name required. Tag unique at the plant. Must be in a unit process. Cannot be its own standby. A tier different from the library's needs a reason.
ReadingName required. Must live on an existing plant, unit process, control group or machine. Valid range low below high (except on/off). Tiers deeper means worse, in the direction set. Every tier inside the valid range. Manual: entry cadence above zero. Computed: expression present; only numbers, + − × ÷, brackets, min max abs sqrt round and identifiers; every identifier bound to an existing reading; not a constant; no self-reference; two formulas cannot feed each other; it computes on probe values.
AlertName and reading required. At least one tier on the reading or as custom limits. Tiers deeper means worse. Tiers inside what the reading can read. An Emergency fires on the first reading (hold 0). A reading-based close needs a back value on the safe side of the entry limit. Clock override: start-within above zero, fix-within longer. Warn when another alert already watches the reading.
ScheduleName required. Cadence above zero. Next due set. A calibration needs its probe. A run-hours basis needs a meter and hours above zero. A vendor needs a name.
Control groupName required. Must be in a unit process. Members only from that unit process.
Manual overrideA reason and an until-date; the date not in the past.
Not installedA reason. Recorded per machine and sensor name; reversible.

7 · Data inputs and formulas

7.1 Data inputs: a value a person enters

A reading with input: "manual". The console sets what the library or the person decides: the cadence (every n shifts, days, weeks or months), who enters it (Operator or Lead), what happens when nobody does (nothing, a Caution, or a Minor issue). The operator app owns the entry screen Manual entry screen; it writes the value as a reading, after which alerts on it behave like any other.

  • A missed entry is an issue of its own: the console exports a missedEntry trigger per manual reading with a missed severity (section 9). The clock is the cadence plus a grace the engine decides (flag F7).
  • A lab value is one sample: alerts on manual readings default to hold 0 and close when the next value is back in band.
  • The library offers data inputs by plant type (inlet and outlet COD, BOD, TSS; for an ETP, TDS and oil and grease; for a WTP, raw turbidity and residual chlorine) and by unit process profile (MLSS and SV30 for a biological stage; head loss for filtration). Each comes with its limits and its alert.

7.2 Formulas: a value the analytical engine computes

A reading with input: "computed". The console stores the definition; the analytical engine Analytical engine evaluates it and writes the result back as that reading, on the same subject. Alerts on it need nothing special.

Grammarnumbers · + - * / · brackets · min max abs sqrt round · identifiers (letters, digits, underscore), each bound to one reading Inputs[{var, readingId}], derived from the identifiers in the expression; bound by the person, or by the library's suggestion when exactly one candidate reading exists Windowlatest (each input's latest value) · 1h · 24h · shift (the average of each input over the window, then the expression) Resulta number with the reading's unit and valid range; outside the range the formula is suspect and the engine raises no alert Subjectany level: a plant-wide removal efficiency, a unit-process SVI, a control group's summed motor current, a machine's specific energy Cyclesa formula may use another formula's result; it may not use its own, and two may not feed each other

Examples the library offers: (inlet_cod - outlet_cod) / inlet_cod * 100 as COD removal on the plant; sv30 * 1000 / mlss as SVI on a biological stage; a WTP's turbidity removal mixing one manual input and one SCADA reading. The console also lets a person write any formula, as the seeded "Average line pressure" on Manesar's blower group shows.

Placeholder. When the engine evaluates (on every input update, or on the window's close), what it does with a missing input (skip the evaluation, write nothing), and whether a formula result feeds another formula in the same cycle, are the analytics build's decisions. The console's contract fixes only the grammar, the window names and the result shape. See flags F5 and F6.

7.3 The funnel: the perfect picture against the actual plant

The library holds the perfect picture of each machine type, stage and plant type: the sensors that should be installed, and every alert, schedule, round, data input and formula that follows from them. A real plant is never the perfect picture: a flow meter was not in the contract, a trip contact was never wired, a stage was built without a probe, a sensor is on order. SCADA reports what is actually installed. The console puts the perfect picture and the actual plant side by side and makes every difference explicit, so that nothing the library expects is silently lost and nothing irrelevant keeps nagging.

  • Compare. Configuration against configuration; no value is read from the plant. For each machine with a library type: the sensor tags the type expects, against the sensor tags installed on that machine as SCADA reports them. For each stage and for the plant: the rounds, data inputs and formulas the profile and the plant type offer, against what has been taken. For each miss, which library alerts, run-hours schedules and calibrations depend on it.
  • Settle each miss one of two ways. Add the sensor tag (the sensor exists, SCADA just did not bring it), prefilled from the library's definition. Or record it as not installed, with a reason, by whom, since when.
  • Consequences of a record. Suggestions that need the sensor move from Blocked to Skipped with the reason shown; they stop counting as pending; the export lists the record under knownGaps; a new library version that still expects the sensor does not re-flag it. Expect it again reverses all of this.
  • What is not compared yet. Connections (readings on pipes and neighbours) and condition-based work are placeholders; stages and groups have no expected-sensor list in the library data yet (flag F15). The funnel is written so those join as more rows, not as a new screen.

8 · The library interface

Freeze this now; the console is built against it and the library build implements it. One read-only, versioned document per library version. The prototype's stub is assets/library.js; the shapes below are its shapes.

                                     manualInputs, formulas, flows, causes, formulaGrammar }

Resolution rules the console applies (the library never names a plant): a unit process gets a profile by keyword match on its zone name; a machine gets a type by SCADA type map or by hand; an equipment-type alert binds to the machine's reading with the same readingKind; a formula input binds to the reading named reading on the target itself, else within the unit process, else anywhere at the plant, and only when exactly one exists. Otherwise the suggestion is blocked and says what it needs.

Status of a suggestion: ready, done (already there), blocked (with why), placeholder (connections, condition-based work). Applying writes source: "library" and templateRef: {level, type|profile, name, version}.

12 · Flags, challenges, questions

Raised as a companion, not as blockers. Each has a recommended default so the build does not wait; say if you disagree.

Decide nowF1 · Freeze the library interface (section 8) this week. Everything the console inherits depends on it. Recommendation: version it as 2026.10, implement it as a JSON file served by the console until the library build exists, and forbid changes to existing fields; additions only.
Decide nowF2 · Who owns control groups. The console lets people define them by hand, and the drawing import derives them from duty and standby pairs. If SCADA already has control groups with their own ids, the import should take them and the console should not allow editing membership. Recommendation: hand-entered now, SCADA-owned when the import contract lands; keep source on the group so the switch is mechanical.
Decide nowF3 · Which flows an alert runs. The console only stores a reference, which is enough for the build. But the operator app cannot be designed until the research says fix steps or guided diagnosis. Recommendation: fix steps now (v2's model), diagnosis as a later flow type with the same reference.
Default takenF4 · Readings on any level. Letting an alert watch any reading at the plant removes the need for v2's connection resolver in this build. The cost: an alert on pump P-1 that watches a header pressure says nothing about why. Recommendation: accept for now; revisit connections when the SCADA import includes pipes.
QuestionF5 · Where the analytical engine runs and how often. Streaming (on every input update) or batch (at each window close)? For latest windows over manual inputs, batch is enough; for 1h over SCADA tags, streaming is costly. Recommendation: evaluate on window close, and on each new value for latest; the console's grammar is the same either way.
QuestionF6 · Formula results that cannot be computed. Division by zero, an input missing for the whole window, an input outside its valid range. Recommendation: the engine writes nothing for that cycle, raises no alert, and the console shows the last computed value with its age. Never raise an alert on a suspect result.
QuestionF7 · The missed-entry clock. "Every day by the Operator" needs a deadline: end of day, end of shift, or cadence plus a grace? Recommendation: end of the cadence window plus two hours of grace, configurable later on the reading.
Default takenF8 · Two-sided limits (pH, temperature bands). A reading has one direction. A band needs two readings or two alerts. Recommendation: two alerts on one reading, with the duplicate warning relaxed when directions differ. Not built yet; cheap to add.
Default takenF9 · Freeze needs the engine's count. The console cannot know how many records an alert has open. Until the engine exposes a count per trigger, the freeze is a no-op and the archive-on-delete rule only fires for seeded items. Recommendation: the engine exposes GET /triggers/{id}/open in its first sprint.
Default takenF10 · Soft delete everywhere, hard delete only for items without records. Archived items leave the lists but keep their history; a plant delete cascades. Recommendation as built; the one thing to confirm is whether a deleted plant must keep its audit for 24 months (yes, I assumed).
QuestionF11 · Lead permissions. The build gives a Lead the cadence of in-house preventive maintenance and nothing else. Site teams may expect a Lead to set limits on readings. Recommendation: keep narrow for launch; widen per plant from User Center later.
QuestionF12 · Sample values versus live values at configuration time. Nothing is read from the plant while configuring; every number on screen is a sample. When SCADA import exists, showing the last value beside a reading would help a person set limits. Recommendation: a "last value and age" field in the import contract, read-only in the console.
Default takenF13 · Plant-level alerts need a subject in the engine. The handoff's Trigger subject was a machine or a stage; this build adds plant and control group. Recommendation: the engine accepts {kind: plant} and {kind: controlGroup, id} as first-class subjects; issues on them show under the plant and under the group's unit process.
Decide nowF14 · What "manual override" means. Built as the console's own switch: a stage, group or machine marked Alerts muted or Under maintenance until a date, suppressing alerts (and pausing schedules) below it. If the intent was SCADA's manual mode (the operator takes a machine off auto), that is a reading the engine should watch, not a console setting. Recommendation: keep the console switch as built, and add SCADA's auto/manual state as a status reading when the import contract lands.
Default takenF15 · Library sets by control group and by stage. The library data has expectations by equipment type, and rounds, inputs and formulas by plant type and profile, but no expected-sensor list for a group or a stage. The funnel compares machines only. Recommendation: the library build adds expects lists to profiles and, when groups are typed, to group types; the console's check grows rows, not screens.
QuestionF16 · When the library changes, does a recorded gap stay recorded? Built: yes, a "not installed" record survives library versions and is reversible. The alternative, re-flagging every miss on each library version, would nag plants about sensors they chose not to install. Recommendation: keep records; show "recorded under library vX" so a reviewer can revisit.
QuestionF17 · Help content ownership. The in-console help (purpose, who may change what, steps, fields, terms, pitfalls) ships as a data file. Who keeps it true as the product moves: product or the library's authors? Recommendation: product owns the screens' help; the library build owns the terms for library concepts; both in the same file, reviewed with every release.

13 · Sequencing

  1. Model and shell. Entities of section 4, CRUD with audit, soft delete and cascade, the shell and the plants list, the persona guard. The library stub from the JSON file. Acceptance: every add, edit, delete in the prototype, with the same validation sentences.
  2. Equipment and readings. Unit processes, control groups, machines (tier, duty, standby); readings with the three inputs, limits, the formula grammar and its validation, the sample computation.
  3. Alerts and schedules. The two drawers, status transitions, inherit (ready, done, blocked, skipped, placeholder; passes), the picker, Reset to library, manual overrides and what they suppress.
  4. Library check. The funnel per plant: expected against present, add-the-tag prefilled from the library, not-installed records with their consequences on suggestions and on the export. The help panel on every screen and drawer.
  5. Review, compile, export. The checklist, go-live, the export of section 9, a contract test against the engine's schema.
  6. Integrations as they land. The library build behind the interface, the engine's open-record count, SCADA import (with control groups and auto/manual state), the analytical engine, the operator app's entry screen. Each replaces a stub without a shape change. People never enter the console; the engine reads User Center.
