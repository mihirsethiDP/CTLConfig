2The model on one page, in plain words

The spine

Plant type›Plant›Stage›Unit process›Equipment›Sensor›Reading

A stage is only a label taken from the unit-process type (Preliminary, Primary, Secondary, Tertiary, Sludge handling): nothing is filed on it. A unit process is a basin or reactor in the plant graph, one object with one page. Each piece of equipment takes part in one or more unit processes, or in none. Control groups sit across the spine: blowers or pumps that feed exactly the same downstream set, holding only their members and a setpoint. Every Trigger, Issue and Task is about one unit process, one piece of equipment or the plant.

Five interaction rules

  1. One editor for each value. A sensor's limits are edited on its page, a Task's cadence on its Trigger page, a design parameter on Details. Every other place shows the value read-only and links there.
  2. Every value shows its source. Library, Derived, Override, Base moved, Gap and the rest, as a chip that never needs a hover.
  3. Any difference from the library is an override, with a reason, an author and a review date, and Deviations lists it.
  4. A new plant is a review. The structure pass, then the bindings pass: automation proposes, a person confirms.
  5. Commissioning, then Live. Nothing is sent while a plant is in Commissioning; Go live starts Issues, notifications and scheduled Tasks.

Alex's six principles, in Alex's order

  1. Nothing changes silently.
  2. Safety.
  3. Show the consequence before saving.
  4. Automation proposes; a person confirms.
  5. One place to edit each thing.
  6. Configuration shows its results.

Where the drafts are silent, v7/2 takes the reading these favour, highest first. Section 5 names the principle behind each reading.

Smaller readings, taken without a call

These come up on fewer screens or change little. Each is built as described, and the principle in brackets decided it.

  • Two blockers for people. "No reachable Operator and Lead" could block only when both are missing; v7/2 shows one blocker per missing role, as Alex's own Readiness drawing does (safety).
  • Undecided drafts. Accepting a binding as a draft does not clear the blocker; activating it, leaving it inactive with a reason or rejecting it does (nothing changes silently).
  • One list of gaps. Alex's three screens list different gaps; v7/2 uses the reference list of four on all of them, and Coverage keeps them (nothing changes silently between screens).
  • The noisy rule against the drawing. One drawing marks DO low noisy at 6 a day; the rule (more than 10 a day) wins, so only Level high · EQ-1 is noisy at Vedanta (one place to edit each thing).
  • The trial's window. Counted from when the Trigger became active in Commissioning, or the latest return, and never under one day, so a few firings in the first hours don't mark a Trigger noisy.
  • No callout for an Emergency trial firing. Alex has none, and adding one would quietly change the model; the row's "Emergency · nobody was told" states the consequence instead (show the consequence).
  • Task bindings. An accepted Task binding is active but generates nothing until go-live; an undecided one is a draft for the blocker.
  • Maintenance programme. Any maintenance Task except calibration counts; the last-service count uses planned maintenance only, because that is what a service date sets.
  • A service date already past its interval. The Task is due on go-live day, never before; compliance counts from go-live.
  • Going live a second time. A Task's last real completion counts as its last service, then the entered date, then go-live day.
  • How drafts on a Live plant are reached. A count on Coverage's Triggers card links to the bindings pass; it is navigation only.
  • Who may go live. "Go live needs Full Admin" is kept as Alex wrote it; the prototype's one user, the Global Admin, can too.
  • Machines and basins. A node is one object, the unit process, with its own page, so "tanks count" holds for the Equipment Card while lists split unit processes from equipment.
  • Participants' Tasks. A unit-process page lists its participants' Issues as shared and counts their Tasks with a link (configuration shows its results).
  • What "Triggers re-checked" means. Triggers on the unit process or on the moved equipment; on a Live plant they are flagged until a person marks them checked (nothing changes silently).
  • When the bindings pass opens. Once every structure row is decided and nothing is left unclassified.
  • Readings as structure rows. One readings row per unit process, Accept only; sensors move only through the survey.
  • Shared participants on the drawing. An outline plus a "shared" tag, with the side panel naming the other unit processes, instead of a dashed copy per group.
  • Stages at a water plant. The stage list is written for wastewater, so Bawal's filters read "Tertiary".
  • Who owns a limit rule. The template; its chip names the template and its version (one place to edit each thing).
  • Where library values are edited. On the template page; a type page opens the same editor in a sheet (one place to edit each thing).
  • When zones are worked out. Whenever a template applies, bound or proposed; a rejected proposal leaves the zone "Gap · no rule".
  • Base moved on a frozen Trigger. Shown, amber, as the pattern for frozen Triggers says.
  • Physical on a constant. The form says no design parameter changes a constant rule and offers only "Override anyway" (show the consequence).
  • Structural "for now". The reason stays Structural, with the one-year review; the drafts give no expiry.
  • Keep and the review date. Keeping an override whose base moved records the new base and a note but leaves the review date alone; Extend is what moves it (nothing changes silently).
  • Similar overrides. Plants that override a field to the same value are grouped with "Consider a library variant?", whatever their reasons, as drawn.
  • Filters on Deviations. The drawing's cluster filter becomes a plant filter, since the study has no clusters.
  • Units on every value. Deviations rows carry units, because cadence rows mix weeks and months.
  • "Notify", not Alex's banned word. Alex's own blocker and go-live copy use a word the word list bans; the word list wins and v7/2 says "notify".
  • What is not built. Bulk overrides and keyboard shortcuts appear on no screen of Alex's, so neither prototype has them.
  • Plant admins on expiry. The prototype has one user, so the Activity line says the plant admins were told.
  • The same moment where Alex's model has no such object. v7/1's chapter 6 is an Issue; in v7/2 it is the Trigger page where the Emergency trial firing is recorded (show the consequence).
  • Version numbers in the story. Chapter text says "the version" and lets each chip say whether it is the type's or the template's.
  • Two vocabularies on a card. Each column of a card speaks its own prototype's words; the page around it uses only words both prototypes allow.
  • Public cards. Alex asked for each disagreement with its document id; the public site shows none, so the cards say what each draft says in plain words.

6What the comparison shows

Commissioning effort

  • v7/2 asks more of the person commissioning a plant. The same twelve moments take more steps: a structure pass before any Trigger is proposed, a sensor page before tuning, people typed in before go-live, a resync after an Issue closes. The action counts in section 3 put a number on it.
  • The structure pass is long and literal. DigitalPaani's has 76 rows. One click accepts the 56 proposed, but the 20 left unclassified must each be placed or set aside by hand, because the membership rules place only blowers, recirculation and backwash pumps, dosing sets and valves. At Bawal the rules alone would leave 7 of its 8 pieces of equipment unclassified. v7/1 asks for one decision per equipment type.
  • Bulk accept can confirm wrong proposals as easily as right ones: the screen chamber as a Collection sump, one control group over both UF trains' backwash pumps, one over both hypo dosing lines.
  • Limits that need a parameter. A level limit can't resolve until a tank height exists, so 3 of DigitalPaani's level readings raise nothing until someone types one; the height then changes nothing, because the sensor already reads % of the tank. Motor-current limits resolve to exactly v7/1's numbers; only the chip changes.
  • Better attribution, and a fix for the screen chamber. Membership points a diagnosis at the right equipment, and a person can change the screen chamber's type to Screening in the console, gaining 14 Task templates without waiting for the database team. v7/1 can only flag it.

Safety

  • Nothing is sent during Commissioning, Emergencies included. Vedanta's pH Emergency on Wed 28 Oct, 15:10 (peak pH 3.7) reached nobody: it is one trial firing. In v7/1 it opened an Issue and told the Leads, with no call and no hooter. One rule, no exceptions, and riskier.
  • New Triggers on a Live plant go straight to real Issues and calls. The only way to get trial evidence first is to return the whole plant: at Manesar that mutes 6 Triggers, stops 9 upcoming Tasks and stops the counts, and going live again waits behind 80 drafts. v7/1 runs only the new Trigger on a trial.
  • The freeze. A Trigger with an open Issue is skipped by every change, a Forced Emergency change included, so the tank with the open low-DO Issue is the one that misses a stricter Emergency limit until the Issue closes, and its Trigger waits for a person to resync it. In v7/1 only the open Issue keeps its old terms.
  • Two of Alex's own screens carry a risk. A probe fault saved as Tuning stays a limit change for 90 days, with no path to a calibration; and the frozen-Trigger preview offers "Close it as a false alarm" to unblock a change, which feeds the false-alarm rate on Coverage.

Library changes and plant notices

  • Quieter for the plants, louder for the publisher. A published change reaches the plants that follow at once, with no note: their Activity stays silent and only the version on the chip changes. In return the publisher gets four kinds, pins and a Downstream count before publishing. v7/1 writes a dated note to each plant a change reaches.
  • Stricter on overrides. A Forced change suspends overrides instead of ending them, so they return when it is lifted, and an override that comes to equal the library clears itself. Base moved, the only amber state, is the one signal a plant gets.
  • More to explain, little extra reach at four plants. Two version numbers, four publish kinds, pins and a Downstream panel that counts plants in overlapping classes; with four plants, nearly every count is 0 or 1.

Day-one work

  • Work piles up on go-live day. No scheduled Task runs before go-live, and every Task without a service date is due that day: at Vedanta 51 of 73 fall due on go-live day even with 4 service dates entered. v7/1 spreads first due dates across each interval.
  • One Tasks-a-day number with every copy in it. About 59 a day at Vedanta against v7/1's grouped 47; about 233 at DigitalPaani with every binding accepted. Filing Tasks on unit processes doesn't shorten the list: each MBBR keeps its own tests and the blower changeover lands on every blower.
  • A fixed Trigger stays noisy. Trial firings are records, so tuning Level high · EQ-1 on Thu 29 Oct leaves its noisy gap until Sat 31 Oct, 01:19, although the preview already says it would have fired 0 times; and DO low · SBR-1 loses v7/1's "mostly short" explanation.

Words

  • One vocabulary with Alex. Screens say Trigger, Zones, Persistence, Override, Base moved, Deviation, survey and plant graph, and Criticality tier A, B or C, and Alex's word list finds no defect.
  • At a cost for operators. "Persistence" and "Clearing" read less plainly than "must hold for" and "must clear for", and "Criticality tier B" less plainly than v6's names; v7/1's name for the plant's own control system, the one staff import from, disappears from the screens.
  • An audit trail from day one. Deviations groups every override by library field with a reason and a review date, and every older value carries the reason Migrated, where v7/1 shows "Reason not recorded".

8What is simulated

  • Readings. Every reading is a sample series, worked out the same way every time from the sensor and the time, as in v7/1. Each count or preview built from them carries Sample readings. DigitalPaani's plant graph is real; its readings are samples too.
  • The survey and identification. DigitalPaani's plant graph is its real layout export, read as the survey's output; identification of unit processes and control groups is worked out from its drawing with the rules as drafted. For the sample plants, the structure is a written seed. "Re-run identification" runs again on the same graph.
  • One user. Everyone is the Global Admin, so role rules ("Go live needs Full Admin") show as text and never block.
  • Phones. "Send code" sends nothing; a labelled sample button stands in for the person entering the code.
  • Messages. Nothing is sent anywhere. An Issue records who would have been told and how.
  • The operator app. Not part of v7/2. Issue and Task pages stand in for the work, and the Equipment Card stands in for the phone screen.
  • Time. One story clock for every plant, from Thu 29 Oct 2026, 09:00 (IST). Move time on runs the engine for every plant.
  • The library. Every type and template at v1, except Sludge blanket high, already at v2 (persistence 5 → 15 min, published Tue 20 Oct).

10How to run and reset

  1. Open site/v7/2/prototype.html in a browser, from any static server or straight from disk. There is no build step and no library: plain JavaScript, with Figtree and IBM Plex Mono from Google Fonts.
  2. Start at All Plants. The Story button opens the 12 chapters; each has "Take me there", "Compare with v7/1" and links to its decisions.
  3. Reset the story (top bar) puts every plant back to Thu 29 Oct, 09:00 and clears everything changed in v7/2's story. It never touches v7/1's story, and it does not clear your calls on the decisions.
