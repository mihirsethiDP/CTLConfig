Why v5

Four asks, 26 October 2026. v5 does these four things and nothing else.

#The askWhat v5 does
1"The detection is not working right. Create a library using the equipment types that we have in our database and keep it restricted to that only. The detection should also be based on that."The library's equipment types are the database's 56 equipment node types, one for one, under the database's own names. A machine's type is the type stored on its node. Nothing is guessed from a label. Nothing asks. The form never changes a type. Detection.
2"We'll work on bifurcating the equipments later."No database type is split. Pump is one type, whatever its form says. Database equipment is not sorted into machines, parts or structures: every database equipment type is an equipment type here.
3"v5 does not cater to unit processes and control groups. It only caters to plant types and equipment types."Two levels: plant type and equipment type. No unit processes and no control groups in the data, the library, the screens, the help or the export. Not in v5.
4"Alerts will be based on the equipment only. Not on the pipe that is coming out of the equipment. E.g. for centrifugal blowers you came up with a 'Line pressure high' alert which cannot be the case in this phase … Clean this up and keep it targeted for the next phase."Every alert and every condition-based job reads a reading on the equipment. Whatever needs a reading on a pipe is kept on its type as "Next phase: connections" and alerts on nothing. The rule.

v5 is built from the current v1: the console at the site root as it stood on 26 October, with its Config and What's enabled tabs, the nine setup steps, the every-machine lists with attention chips, and the library fed by SCADA's equipment form. v1 stays as it is. v5 lives in /v5/, has its own copy of every file, and saves under its own key, so the two never share data.

The equipment-type, plant-type, reading, form-rule, new-type and to-confirm tables on this page, and the counts beside them, are generated from the files the v5 prototype runs (assets/catalogue.js and assets/flows.js), not typed by hand. The other tables were written by hand against the running prototype. If the page and the prototype disagree, the prototype is right and the page is stale.

The library: plant types and the database's equipment types

Two levels. Nothing between them.

1 · Plant typeSTP, ETP, WTP. Gives the plant-wide rounds and milestones. Lists the equipment types such a plant usually has, as a guide. It adds no machine and no alert.
2 · Equipment typeThe database's 56 equipment node types, one for one, in the database's order, under the database's names with their typos kept (Softner, Cass Basin, Vtg Chamber, QUADSON EVAPORATOR). Each gives the readings a machine of the type has on it, alerts on those readings with limits, schedules with cadence, a monthly calibration for each probe, its flows and causes, and what it reads from the SCADA form. What needs a connection waits on the type, in plain sight.

In numbers: 56 equipment types, each with a standard set (49 alerts and 61 schedules in all). 39 readings: 23 on the equipment, 16 on a connection. 22 types keep something for the next phase. 100 flows (17 diagnostic, 83 action) and 62 causes, all written for database types, the whole plant, or any equipment. 14 more flows and 12 causes are written and kept aside, not installed, because each needs a reading on a connection: 10 diagnostics for alerts that wait on a type, 2 plant-wide diagnostics (an odour complaint, outlet BOD or COD high) that only an alert on the plant's inlet or outlet could open, the turbidity meter calibration and the chlorine dose adjustment, and the causes only those flows reach. 24 types carry a muted to confirm chip: their content, or a limit in it, is a reasoned guess for the owner to check. Every equipment type

56 rows, in the database's order. Reads on the equipment is what a machine of the type usually has on it. Alerts and Schedules are the type's standard set. Next phase is what was taken out because it needs a reading on a connection. A machine inherits an alert only when SCADA drew the reading it needs; otherwise the alert waits and the screens say which reading is missing. Three kinds of alert behave differently:

  • Only where SCADA draws the reading. An open tank's pH high, pH low and Sump flooding. Without a pH probe or a flood switch they are simply not part of that machine: not waiting, not missing, never amber.
  • Only while the machine runs. UV intensity low, and a VFD pump's Running below minimum speed: a stopped machine reads zero, so the alert watches only while its Run status is ON.
  • On/off alerts (a trip, a flood switch, a lamp fault, a chlorine leak) fire when the contact goes ON, the moment it reaches its limit of 1.

What the library reads from SCADA's form

The form's answers add limits, work and notes to a machine, and a pump on a VFD also gets a Running below minimum speed alert, which watches only while the pump runs. They never change its type. A motor rating puts the current limits at 100%, 105% and 115% of full load (about 1.82 A per kW at 415 V), widens the reading's valid range to twice full load so a real overload is never taken for a sensor fault, and on a Blower moves the intake filter job to 95% of full load, done below 85%. v5 adds no question of its own to the database's form.

Pipes

Pipes are read so the drawing shows what feeds what. In v5 they carry no reading and no alert. DigitalPaani has 96 connections.

Detection: one rule

The stored equipment type wins over the shape drawn. A machine's type is the equipment type stored on its SCADA node. When none is stored, it is the code of the shape it was drawn with. The library entry with that code is the machine's type, under the database's name.

That is all of it. Nothing is guessed from a label or a name. Nothing asks. No answer is remembered for a code. The SCADA form is read for what it says about the machine; a form answer never changes the type. Two edge cases:

  • A drawing element (Plant, ZONE, Joint, Building) is not a machine.
  • A code the database does not have becomes a machine marked not in the database. It inherits nothing, and step 9 says so. It should not happen.

Alerts on the equipment only

An alert reads a reading on the equipment. Never the pipe coming out of it. The same holds for condition-based work: a job that starts when a reading passes a value. Calendar schedules and run-hour schedules are fine.

A reading is on the equipment when its sensor sits on or in the machine and measures the machine's own state or what it holds: run status, trip, auto/manual, motor current, speed, run hours, vibration, bearing or winding temperature, valve position, lamp fault or UV intensity, level in a tank, a flood switch, a probe in the tank's contents (DO, pH, MLSS, ORP, temperature), sludge blanket, the level or weight of a chemical container. A chemical tank's level belongs to the Dosing Tank, which carries Chemical tank low; the Dosing Pump that draws from it carries no tank level.

A reading is on a connection when it measures the water in a pipe, or a difference between two connections: line, discharge, suction or inlet pressure; flow and totals; differential pressure across a filter; pressure across a membrane; permeability; recovery; outlet quality; level across a screen; cooling water in and out. These come with the next phase. So does a reading of the water linked to a machine whose type does not carry it (a temperature on a Blower, a pH on a Dosing Pump), with the note "read in the water around it, not on the machine; comes with connections".

Next phase: connections — Line pressure (on the discharge line) · alert Line pressure high

  • It is grey, with a dashed chip. Never amber.
  • It has no limits and no button, and clicking it does nothing. A read-only sheet for it is built (its value, what it is, where it is, which next-phase alerts wait for it, "Limits and alerts on connections come with the next phase", only Close), but no screen opens it yet.
  • It is never offered when adding an alert, a condition-based job, a flow's reading step, a computed reading's inputs, or a library alert. Typing its tag into a formula is refused, with the reason.
  • It is never a gap. It never shows as "limits missing" or "not enabled yet". What's enabled lists it under "Comes with the next phase".
  • The runtime refuses to fire an alert on it. The export lists it, per machine, under what waits for connections.

The blower's Line pressure high

v1's Centrifugal blower watched Line pressure (Minor above 0.65 bar, Major above 0.85 bar), and Manesar had it active on BL-1 as its open issue. The pressure is measured on the discharge line, a connection. In v5:

  • The Blower type reads Run status, Trip contact, Motor current and Run hours. Its alerts are Motor current high (Caution above 44 A, Minor above 48 A) and Blower tripped (Emergency, when the trip contact goes ON). A motor rating on the form replaces 44 and 48 A with limits worked out from full load.
  • Line pressure and Line pressure high sit in the Blower's Next phase: connections box: "on the discharge line" and "needs the discharge line, a connection".
  • Manesar's BL-1 still shows its Line pressure widget, muted. Its open issue is now Motor current high at 49 A, Minor. BL-1 and BL-2 are 22 kW blowers on their forms (40 A full load), so 44 A and 48 A are 110% and 120% of full load and its usual 38.2 A is normal.
  • The Blower line pressure high diagnostic is written and kept aside with the other flows that wait for connections, listed on the Library's Database tab under "Flows waiting for connections".

17 sample widgets sit on a connection and arrive marked next phase. Bawal: BS-1 level differential, MGF-1 differential pressure, SF-1 throughput, UF-1 recovery and inlet pressure, DP-1 residual chlorine. Vedanta: BL-1 and BL-2 line pressure.

The plant has no alerts of its own: an alert on the plant would read its inlet or outlet, which are connections. Plant-wide rounds and milestones stay.

Not in v5, and what the next phase brings

Not in v5What that means here
Unit processesNo stages, no process areas, no unit-process library, rounds, alerts or criticality rules. A plant is its plant type and its machines. Zones in the sample drawings are ignored.
Control groupsNo duty/standby pairs, no group alerts, no standby tiers. A machine's tier comes from its type, or an override with a reason.
Splitting a database typePump is one type. Its form's Type (MonoBlock, Screw Pump, Centrifugal Pump …) is noted on the machine; a Screw Pump answer adds a stator and rotor check, the way a cutter pump gets a cutter check.
What a tank or pump is forNo Service question. Equalisation, aeration, chlorine contact and the like are unit processes by another name, so v5 neither asks for them nor keeps them on a machine, a chip or the export.
ConnectionsPipes are drawn and read, but carry no reading and no alert. Every reading on a pipe, and every alert or job that needs one, waits on its type.

The next phase: connections

What already waits for it, in the prototype:

  • 22 equipment types with readings, alerts or jobs in their Next phase: connections box. The library table above lists each one.
  • 16 readings classified as on a connection, each with the reason.
  • 14 flows written and kept aside: 10 diagnostics for alerts that wait on a type, among them Blower line pressure high, Filter differential pressure high and UF recovery low or pressure high; the 2 plant-wide diagnostics, Odour complaint and Outlet BOD or COD high, which only an alert on the plant's inlet or outlet could open; the turbidity meter calibration; and the chlorine dose adjustment.
  • 12 causes only those flows reach, among them a weir out of level, UF fibre breakage and hypochlorite that has lost its strength. The Library's Database tab lists them under "Causes waiting for connections".
  • 17 sample widgets already drawn on connections, waiting to be used. A Cooling Tower's water temperature, and any reading of the water linked to a machine whose type does not carry it, wait the same way.
  • Form rules that need a connection: Maximum Head (discharge pressure limits on pumps), OBR (the softener's regeneration point by volume), Design TMP (the MBR's pressure limits), and auto valves that regenerate on volume or backwash on pressure.

How connections are modelled is not decided here.

26 Oct 2026: what changed

v5, built from the current v1 the same day. What changed against v1, in a line or two each.

  • The library is the database's equipment types. 56 types, one per database equipment node type, under its own names and order. v1's 41 types are gone; their content moved onto the database types they describe. The library.
  • Detection is one rule. The stored equipment type wins over the shape drawn. No guessing from names, no pump question, no type select, no remembered answers; the form never changes a type. Detection.
  • Drawing elements are not machines. Plant, ZONE, Joint and Building are counted on one line and inherit nothing. Valves are machines. Pipes are followed through joints. Drawing elements.
  • Alerts on the equipment only. Every reading is classified as on the equipment or on a connection. Connection items moved to each type's "Next phase: connections" box; connection widgets arrive muted and never alert. The rule.
  • No unit processes, no control groups. Steps 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8, the equipment tree, What's enabled and the Causes tab are grouped by equipment type; each flow says which equipment types it is written for, or the whole plant. The help is rewritten without stage, zone, pair or standby words. The screens.
  • The library page has three tabs: Equipment types, Plant types, Database.
  • Flows: 100 installed (17 diagnostic, 83 action) and 62 causes, all by database type; 14 flows and 12 causes kept aside for the next phase.
  • Own storage. v5 saves under ctl5.estate.v1 and loads its files with ?v=1. v1 is untouched.

After review, the same day

  • On/off alerts fire. A trip, a flood switch, a lamp fault or a chlorine leak fires when the contact goes ON; before, a contact at 1 never crossed a limit of 1. The export writes these as == 1.
  • No Service question. It listed unit processes under another name. Gone from every form, chip, card and the export.
  • The Dosing Pump carries no tank level. Chemical tank low lives on the Dosing Tank's Level only.
  • Alerts that fit the machine. An open tank's pH and flooding apply only where SCADA draws the reading. UV intensity low and Running below minimum speed watch only while the machine runs. pH and MLSS each have a high and a low alert in the library (a machine gets the high one today; open questions).
  • Water readings. A Cooling Tower's temperature widget is its cooling water, a connection. A water reading linked to a machine whose type does not carry it waits for the next phase.
  • Current limits from the motor rating widen the reading's valid range, so a big motor's real overload opens an issue instead of being refused as a sensor fault.
  • Manesar's blowers are 22 kW on their forms, which fits their 44 A and 48 A limits. The Blower's intake filter job starts at 42 A, below the Caution, and the motor rating tunes it.
  • Logbook rounds read no sensor and fit any plant; a WTP has its own.
  • Flows and causes set aside. The two plant-wide diagnostics, the chlorine dose adjustment and the 12 causes only next-phase flows reach moved out of the installed library: 100 flows and 62 causes are installed. The outlet BOD or COD diagnostic now asks about the clarifier weir.
  • Database wording kept. The question-file-only codes are shown by code, with no invented names. The Oil & Grease Tank, MBBR and Tube settler forms ask "Breath", as the database does, and the volume reads it.
  • To confirm. 24 types carry a muted chip with the line the owner should check. Submersible Pump service moved to 4000 run-hours; the tonner reading is its net weight.
  • Readings on the plant are lab values recorded in rounds: they take no limits and are never a gap. The manual reading sheet refuses a connection reading's name.
  • Screens. Step 3 says when the stored type wins over the drawn one. What's enabled can show one machine. Step 1 can remove an empty plant. The person sheet refuses an edit or a removal only when it would leave the plant without the reachable Operator or Lead it had, and says so on the sheet. The help for step 8, the equipment page and the manual reading sheet matches the screens.
