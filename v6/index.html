2 · Root causes and their equipment

Any sludge pump means Pump and Submersible Pump. "Dosing system" means Dosing Pump and Dosing Tank. "Motor / MCC" means the issue's own machine: it is that machine's motor. A cell that names a part with no database type (a rake, a gearbox, a drive chain, a wiper, a conveyor, a diffuser, the air piping, the blower room) keeps the text as the root cause's part, shown as a chip. 3 · One diagnostic per issue

Each issue has one diagnostic. It asks one question per root cause, in the file's order:

"Is this the cause? <the root cause>". Yes lands on that root cause and offers its fix.
No goes to the next root cause.
After the last No: "None of these: describe what you found".

No checks were invented. An alert that opens the issue, and an operator who reports it, both walk the same questions. 4 root causes close as a sensor fault: the number was wrong, not the plant.

How often. The Frequency cell is read as written. Hours are new: a schedule can run every few hours, at a time of day. "Whichever is earlier" means calendar or run-hours, whichever comes first. A range takes its shorter end and keeps the range in the note. A condition ("during peak flow", "if DO is less than 1 ppm", "can be avoided if automated") is kept word for word in the note. A cell with two or three jobs becomes one schedule per job, sharing the task's steps. A test that depends on a lab runs at the no-lab cadence, every 30 days, with the lab cadence in the note. Notes are shown with the schedule, on step 7, the machine page, the Tasks tab and the schedule sheet.

Run-hours. On a machine with a run-hours reading, "every 150 hours of operation" runs on run-hours alone. Its due date is an estimate from the hours left, at 20 run-hours a day, with no time of day; the month plan marks each one ≈. The export gives it no calendar rule, only a floor: never more often than the run-hours would take at 24 hours a day, at least a day and at most 30 days. That is 6 days for 150 run-hours and 20 days for 500. The schedule sheet states the same floor. A machine without a run-hours reading runs the job on clock hours, with a note saying so.
